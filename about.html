The Public Element is a space dedicated to making civic and political engagement more accessible to students and young people, but its content and resources are open to everybody wanting to learn, connect, and engage with world-shaping decisions at State and Federal level alike.

Politics can often feel distant, complicated, or like something happening somewhere else and in which we do not have much of a say at all. But many of the decisions that shape our everyday lives — from education and transportation to housing, climate policy, and community resources — are influenced by the government, and public participation of individuals like you!

The Public Element is designed to help students better understand how civic institutions work, stay informed about issues that affect them, and discover ways to participate both on campus and in their communities. You do not need to be a political expert to have a voice. Civic engagement starts with understanding what is happening around you, asking questions, and choosing to get involved.

THE AUTHOR

Hi, I’m Lee, the creator of The Public Element. As a student involved in campus leadership and civic engagement, I have seen how much influence students can have when they understand how institutions work and feel confident using their voices.


I created The Public Element to help make politics and civic participation feel more approachable — especially for young people who may want to get involved but are not sure where to begin, and for those who feel disconnected from civic engagement. My goal is to encourage students to become informed, participate in their communities, and recognize that civic engagement can take many forms. With The Public Element, I’m hoping to connect youth with student government resources, public meeting guidelines, volunteering opportunities, advocacy training, and more!


Besides being the mind behind The Public Element, I am currently Ohlone College's Associated Student Body's President, pursuing a degree in Environmental Studies. My dream is to find a job in policy that can support the environmental cause and contribute to a more just, cleaner, healthier planet for all its inhabitants.
